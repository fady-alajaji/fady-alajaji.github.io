Research Interests

Keywords: Information theory, joint source-channel coding, digital communications, error-control coding, data compression, applied probability.

My primary research interests belong to the general area of information and communication theory. More specifically, I am interested in the coding of information-bearing signals for transmission over noisy communication networks.

One objective is to understand and investigate the Shannon theoretic aspects of this problem, namely to determine the fundamental limits of how efficiently one can encode information and still recover it with negligible loss. Another objective is to develop efficient coding techniques and algorithms for achieving reliable data transmission over wireless networks.

I also have a long-standing interest in probability problems and their applications, including random processes with reinforcement, contagion models and generalized Polya urns, stochastic modelling and analysis of network epidemics, game theory, and effective bounds for the probability of a union of events under partial information.

Current Research Activities

  • Information Theory: Properties of information measures and their operational characterizations for systems with memory; information spectrum techniques; Shannon coding theorems, source and channel coding; error probability, cutoff rates and reliability functions; capacity and capacity-cost functions; feedback capacity of channels with memory; two-way networks; information hiding; channels with memory and correlated fading channels.
  • Joint Source-Channel Coding: Channel optimized vector quantization; clustering algorithms; source-optimized channel codes; wireless communications and sensor networks; source-channel signal mapping and modulation; MAP joint source-channel decoding; Turbo codes, LDPC codes and iterative decoding; hybrid digital-analog signaling; MIMO channels and space-time coding.
  • Applied Probability: Contagion-based models and generalized Polya urns.
